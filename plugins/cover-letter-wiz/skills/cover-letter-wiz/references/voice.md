# Voice: sounding like a person who wanted this job

Read this before drafting. It is the part of the skill most likely to fail,
because the failure is invisible from the inside. A letter can be accurate,
well organized, correctly formatted, and still get skimmed and discarded
because it reads like software wrote it.

## Punctuation

Hard bans, no exceptions:

- **Em dash (—) and en dash (–).** The single most recognizable tell. If a
  sentence wants one, it is two sentences, or it wants a comma, or it wants
  parentheses. Rewrite until it wants none of them.
- **Colon (:).** Including in the salutation, which takes a comma instead.
  A colon in a cover letter almost always introduces a list, and the list is
  usually the robotic part. Cut the list or make it a sentence.
- **Semicolon (;).** Nobody writing a sincere letter reaches for one.

Allowed and encouraged: periods, commas, question marks used sparingly,
apostrophes. Contractions are fine and help. Hyphens in compound words
(part-time, follow-up) are fine, they are not dashes.

## Banned openings

Never open with any of these, or a paraphrase:

- "I am writing to express my interest in..."
- "I am excited to apply for..."
- "I was thrilled to see your posting for..."
- "As a seasoned professional with X years of experience..."
- "When I saw the posting for X, I knew I had to apply."
- Anything that opens by naming the posting and the job board it came from.

The first sentence is the only one guaranteed to be read. Spend it on
something only this applicant could say. Start mid-thought if that works.

## Banned words and phrases

leverage · utilize · spearhead · passionate about · proven track record ·
align with · resonate with · delve into · tapestry · testament to · wealth
of experience · uniquely positioned · seamlessly · robust · dynamic ·
synergy · pivotal · crucial · myriad · plethora · navigate the complexities ·
in today's fast-paced world · at the intersection of · deep dive · move the
needle · hit the ground running · wear many hats · think outside the box ·
I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my skills can contribute to ·
I am confident that my background makes me an ideal candidate

Also avoid stacking adjectives before a noun. "A collaborative, results
driven, detail oriented approach" says nothing. One concrete noun beats
three adjectives.

## Banned structures

- **"Not just X, but Y."** Also "It is not only X, it is Y." Instant tell.
- **Rule of three.** Three parallel items in a row, three parallel clauses,
  three parallel sentences. Use two, or four, or an uneven list.
- **Rhetorical questions.** "What does that mean for your team?" No.
- **Perfect paragraph symmetry.** If every paragraph is four sentences of
  similar length, break it. Vary paragraph length. One paragraph can be two
  sentences.
- **Perfect sentence symmetry.** Vary sentence length hard. A long sentence
  that builds through a couple of clauses, then a short one. Like that.
- **Echoing the posting's headings back.** If the posting has a
  "Qualifications" section, do not write a paragraph that walks its bullets
  in order. That is the robotic repeat the letter is supposed to avoid.
- **Restating the resume.** The resume already lists what was done. The
  letter says why it mattered, what it was like, or what it led to.
- **"In conclusion" / "In closing."** Just close.

## What to do instead

- Prefer plain verbs. Used, ran, built, fixed, wrote, taught, sold, cut.
- One specific number or proper noun does more work than any adjective.
  Not "significantly improved attendance" but "took the Saturday sessions
  from nine people to forty."
- Say the awkward true thing rather than the smooth vague thing. "I have not
  worked in procurement, but I have written the justifications that
  procurement kicked back twice" is more persuasive than any claim of being
  detail oriented.
- Read it aloud in your head. If a sentence is hard to say, it is hard to
  read.
- It is fine for the letter to be short. Three tight paragraphs beat five
  padded ones.

## Human flair

Every letter needs at least one passage that could not appear in anyone
else's letter for this job. This is a requirement, not a garnish.

Source it in this order:

1. **The user's notes (input 2).** This is what they are for. Find the most
   specific, most personal, least resume-like thing in there and build a
   sentence or two around it. A detail about why they want this particular
   job, a connection to the place, an opinion, a story, a person they met.
2. **A specific detail from the resume that the resume states flatly.** An
   unusual location, an odd credential, a project with a real name, a
   pairing of two things that rarely go together. Give it the color the
   resume had no room for.
3. **The posting itself,** only if it says something specific enough to
   react to honestly.

Rules for flair:

- It must be true. Never invent a story, a motivation, an opinion, or a
  connection. Everything traces back to the resume, the notes, or the
  posting.
- It goes in the body where it supports a claim, or in the opening. Do not
  bolt it onto the end as a personality paragraph.
- One good one is enough. Three is trying too hard.
- If the notes are empty and nothing in the resume gives you anything real
  to work with, say so in your reply to the user and ask for one detail
  rather than inventing one.

## Before and after

**Opening.**

Before: I am writing to express my strong interest in the Program Analyst
position. With a proven track record in community economic development, I am
confident I would be a valuable addition to your team.

After: For the past two years I have run budget workshops for people who
had never opened a spreadsheet before. That is most of what a program
analyst does, minus the folding chairs.

**Body claim.**

Before: I leveraged my analytical skills to spearhead a robust outreach
initiative, resulting in significantly increased engagement across multiple
stakeholder groups.

After: I rebuilt the outreach list by hand after finding that half the
contacts were dead ends. Attendance at the monthly sessions went from nine
to forty over the following quarter.

**Close.**

Before: I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my skills and
experience can contribute to the continued success of your organization.

After: I would be glad to talk about the role. My hours are odd because of
the time difference, so evenings your time are easiest.

## Final read-through

Before building the file, reread the draft against this list:

1. Search the text for `—`, `–`, `:`, and `;`. Zero hits outside the header
   contact line.
2. Does the first sentence name the posting or announce that the writer is
   applying? Rewrite it.
3. Is there a "not just, but"? A rule of three? A rhetorical question?
4. Pick any sentence at random. Could it appear in a letter from a different
   applicant for a different job? If most sentences could, the letter has no
   flair yet.
5. Does any paragraph walk the resume in order? Cut it.
6. Read the whole thing aloud. Fix whatever you stumble on.
